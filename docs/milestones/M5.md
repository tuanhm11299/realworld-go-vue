# M5 · Favorites, feed, and pagination

**Tests:** `.specs/api/hurl/favorites.hurl`, `.specs/api/hurl/feed.hurl`,
`.specs/api/hurl/pagination.hurl`

**Goal:** aggregates and joins at list scale — and the first time you make a
performance decision with evidence instead of instinct.

## Why this milestone exists

Every endpoint so far returned one thing, or a small list you could afford to
build naively. The feed will not let you: it joins the social graph to articles,
counts favourites, resolves authors and tags, and does it for every row. This is
where "it works" and "it works at scale" stop being the same sentence.

## Build

1. **Favourite / unfavourite** — `POST` and `DELETE`
   `/api/articles/{slug}/favorite`. Both return the article with an updated
   `favorited` and `favoritesCount`. Idempotent, same as following.

2. **`favoritesCount`** — decide: a `COUNT(*)` over `favorites` at read time, or
   a denormalised counter column on `articles` kept in step by the write. Write
   down the trade-off (correctness and simplicity versus read cost) and pick one.
   If you denormalise, what keeps it honest?

3. **Feed** — `GET /api/articles/feed`, auth required: articles by authors the
   caller follows, newest first, with `limit` and `offset`. Watch your routing:
   `feed` must not be captured by the `{slug}` pattern.

4. **Now fix the N+1 you found in M4.** In order:
   ```sql
   EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS) <your feed query>;
   ```
   Read the plan. Find the sequential scans and the row estimates that are wildly
   wrong. Add the index you predicted back in M1, re-run, and compare. Only then
   consolidate the per-article queries — as a `LEFT JOIN` with aggregation, or
   `json_agg`, or two queries (articles, then everything for that page's ids in
   one `WHERE id = ANY($1)`). All three are legitimate; the last is often the
   most readable.

5. **Pagination** — defaults `limit=20`, `offset=0`, and `articlesCount` is the
   total, not the page size. Reject a negative or absurd `limit` rather than
   passing it to Postgres.

## Verify

```bash
.specs/api/run-api-tests-hurl.sh .specs/api/hurl/favorites.hurl
.specs/api/run-api-tests-hurl.sh .specs/api/hurl/feed.hurl
.specs/api/run-api-tests-hurl.sh .specs/api/hurl/pagination.hurl
```

Then, for real: seed 100k articles and 1M favourites with `generate_series`, and
time the feed before and after your index. Keep the numbers.

## Questions to answer in writing

- How many queries does one feed request make now? How many did it make before?
- Which index did you add, and what does the plan look like with and without it?
  Paste both.
- `OFFSET 10000` — what does Postgres actually do to satisfy that? (It walks and
  discards 10,000 rows.) What would a cursor-based alternative look like, and why
  does the RealWorld spec not use one?
- If you denormalised `favoritesCount`: what happens if a favourite insert
  succeeds and the counter update fails?

## Going further

- Add `pgx` query tracing behind a flag so you can count queries per request in
  the log. You will reach for this constantly later.
- Benchmark the endpoint with `k6` or `hey` and record p50/p95/p99. This becomes
  your baseline for Stage C of the roadmap.
