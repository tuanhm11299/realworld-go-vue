# M3 · Profiles and following

**Tests:** `.specs/api/hurl/profiles.hurl`, `.specs/api/hurl/errors_profiles.hurl`

**Goal:** the social graph, and the concept of a response that changes shape
depending on who is asking.

## Why this milestone exists

`GET /api/profiles/:username` is the first endpoint with **optional**
authentication: anonymous callers get `following: false`, authenticated callers
get the truth. That pattern — one resource, viewer-dependent fields — runs
through every remaining endpoint, so it is worth isolating here where the payload
is four fields rather than fifteen.

## Build

1. `GET /api/profiles/{username}` behind `MaybeAuthenticate`. Unknown username is
   `404`.
2. `POST /api/profiles/{username}/follow` and its `DELETE`, both behind
   `Authenticate`, both returning the updated profile.
3. Make both writes **idempotent**. Following someone you already follow must
   succeed. Do it in the database (`ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, `DELETE` that
   matches nothing) rather than with a read-then-write, which races.

## Questions to answer in writing

- Where does the `following` flag come from — a second query per profile, or a
  `LEFT JOIN` in the first one? At one profile it does not matter. At a list of
  twenty article authors it is the difference between 1 query and 21. Which will
  you need in M4, and does that change your answer here?
- You have `MaybeAuthenticate` and `Authenticate`. What does the first do with a
  token that is present but *invalid* — treat the caller as anonymous, or reject
  with 401? Both are defensible. Which did the tests want, and why?
- Can a user follow themselves through your API? Should the block live in the
  handler, the domain, or the schema?
